OpenAY Mic turns an Android phone into a studio-grade microphone for your desktop computer. The phone captures audio and streams it to a receiver that presents it as a native audio device: on Linux a PipeWire virtual microphone that every application can select, on Windows audio rendered through WASAPI (pair it with a virtual-audio-cable driver to expose it as a microphone).

Transports: Wi-Fi (UDP), USB (TCP over adb), and Bluetooth SPP. Audio: 16-bit PCM at 48 kHz or Opus in 10 ms low-delay frames, carried over a frozen, published wire protocol. The receiver runs an adaptive jitter buffer; end-to-end software-path latency measures p50 around 47 ms with default settings, at under 1 percent CPU when idle.

Set up in three steps: install this app, run the receiver on your computer (get it from the project's GitHub releases page), connect over the same Wi-Fi network or with a USB cable, then press ON AIR.

Free and open source under the MIT license. No cloud, no accounts, no tracking: your audio stays on your local network, and the receiver latches onto the first peer it accepts so nothing else can blend into the stream.

For streamers, podcasters, and tinkerers who want a real microphone without extra hardware.
